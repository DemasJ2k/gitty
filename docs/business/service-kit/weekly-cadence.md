# Weekly cadence and the anti-"build instead of sell" rule

The council was blunt: the one thing most likely to sink this is you, a
technical founder, building a tool instead of selling. This file is the guardrail.

## The rule

**Hit your outbound quota before you write a line of code or polish anything.**
Building feels productive. It is not progress until you have a paying client.

## Weekly quota (adjust the numbers, keep the discipline)

Set a fixed, non-negotiable weekly minimum. A workable default:

- **50 new prospects contacted** (touch 1), or
- **80–100 total touches** across new + follow-ups, whichever you prefer.
- **Every open thread followed up** on schedule (touches 2–7).

Log every touch in `pipeline-tracker.csv`. If the quota is not hit, nothing else
happens that week.

## Two-week checkpoint

At the end of week two, judge the pipeline, not the revenue:

- **Zero revenue at week two:** normal. Deals take 2–6 weeks to close.
- **Zero conversations at week two:** the real failure signal. Fix the input,
  not the product. Usually one of: too few touches, a weak/ generic audit
  finding, wrong decision-maker, or a pitch that leads with you instead of their
  gap.

Do not respond to a slow week by improving a tool. Respond by improving the
audit finding, the subject line, or the number of touches.

## Simple weekly rhythm

- **Mon:** add 25–50 new prospects to the list; run their audits.
- **Tue–Thu:** send touch-1s and all scheduled follow-ups; take any calls.
- **Fri:** review the tracker — touches sent, replies, calls booked, closes.
  Note what to change next week.

## When to allow yourself to build

Only after **3+ paying clients** and you notice you are doing the same manual
audit/report steps every week. That repetition is the signal to productize
(reusing your app's RAG + encrypted-key pattern, per the decision doc) — not
before.
